Eclipse‑Timing Analysis Background

Eclipsing Binary Star Systems

Eclipsing binaries are multi‑star systems whose orbital plane lies nearly edge‑on along our line of sight. As the two stars revolve around one another, they periodically eclipse each other, producing regular, repeating dips in the observed brightness. These systems serve as powerful astrophysical laboratories: precise measurements of their orbits enable us to determine fundamental stellar parameters and advance our understanding of stellar evolution and internal stellar physics.

Causes of Orbital‑Period Variations

The orbital periods of eclipsing binaries are not always perfectly constant. Apparent or real period variations can arise from several physical mechanisms: stellar mass transfer between the binary components, mass loss via stellar winds, tidal interactions, the light‑travel time effect induced by an unseen tertiary companion, and stellar magnetic activity cycles. Measuring these subtle period changes with high precision is critical. It allows us to constrain the evolutionary history of the binary, reconstruct its mass transfer record, and search for hidden third bodies in the system.

Research Objective of This Project

In this work, we perform eclipse‑timing analysis on the eclipsing binary TIC 233720276 using high‑cadence photometric data from the TESS mission. We derive accurate mid‑eclipse times, construct an O‑C diagram over a 5.33‑year baseline, and test whether a constant‑period ephemeris fits both eclipse types or whether the primary and secondary eclipses drift apart — a signature that would point beyond a simple change in the orbital period.
