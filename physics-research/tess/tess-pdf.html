Eclipse‑Timing Analysis of TIC 233720276 Using TESS Observations

About This Research Work

This work carries out eclipse‑timing analysis for the eclipsing binary star TIC 233720276 based on NASA TESS space‑telescope photometric observations. I processed 41 sectors of 120‑second cadence light‑curve data spanning 5.33 years, from August 2019 to December 2024. Using Python packages including Lightkurve, Astropy, SciPy and NumPy, I performed data quality filtering, light‑curve normalisation, and eclipse template‑fitting to extract precise mid‑eclipse moments.

In total, 407 primary‑eclipse timings and 415 secondary‑eclipse timings were obtained for further analysis. For primary eclipses, a refined constant orbital period of approximately 2.2 days gives good agreement with observations; the O‑C residual standard deviation is only 1.810 seconds. Primary‑eclipse timings show no long‑term drift above that level across the whole observational time span.

However, the secondary eclipse exhibits significant systematic temporal shift relative to the primary eclipse. After removing its static phase‑0.5 offset, the secondary eclipse moved from occurring roughly 88 seconds ahead to about 78 seconds behind the prediction — 167 seconds in total over the 5.33‑year baseline, corresponding to an apparent shift rate of approximately 31.4 seconds per year (R² = 0.999 across the 41 sector medians, against a median secondary timing error of 3.6 seconds). Since primary‑eclipse timings remained stable, a simple overall orbital‑period change cannot explain this behaviour.

Potential physical explanations include apsidal motion of an eccentric orbit, star‑spot activity, or systematic shape‑related fitting effects. At present, we cannot fully rule out data‑processing and template‑fitting artefacts. Future work will incorporate historical eclipse timings, test multiple eclipse‑fitting models, and separate SAP / PDCSAP datasets to constrain the true physical origin of this secondary‑eclipse shift.
